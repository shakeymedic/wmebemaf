NICE Guideline Summary:

Management of New-Onset or Acute Atrial Fibrillation in the ED

Based on general NICE principles for UK Emergency Medicine Practice (NG196)

Initial Assessment & Management

Assess Haemodynamic Stability

If unstable (shock, syncope, myocardial ischaemia, severe heart failure): Emergent electrical cardioversion. Seek expert help immediately.

Identify & Treat Reversible Causes

Consider sepsis, electrolyte abnormalities (K+, Mg2+), thyrotoxicosis, alcohol excess, post-operative state.

Treat underlying cause. AF may resolve spontaneously. If AF persists, manage as per guidelines.

Initial Investigations

12-lead ECG (confirm AF, check for ischaemia, LVH, bundle branch block, WPW).

Bloods: FBC, U&Es, TFTs, LFTs, coagulation screen (if considering anticoagulation). Troponin if clinically indicated (chest pain/ischaemia suspected).

CXR if respiratory symptoms or signs of heart failure.

Rhythm vs. Rate Control Decision

Discuss options with the patient, considering their preferences, symptoms, and comorbidities.

Offer Rhythm Control If:
  • AF onset < 48 hours.
  • AF is causing haemodynamic instability (emergent cardioversion).
  • Reversible cause identified and corrected, but AF persists.
  • Patient preference for rhythm control and it's clinically appropriate.
  • New-onset AF.
  • Symptoms are unacceptable despite rate control.
Offer Rate Control If:
  • AF onset > 48 hours or uncertain, and stable.
  • Patient has no symptoms or minimal symptoms of AF.
  • Significant structural heart disease (where rhythm control may be less successful or risky without specialist input).
  • Patient preference for rate control.

Rhythm Control Strategy

Anticoagulation for Cardioversion:

  • AF onset < 48 hours & low stroke risk (CHA₂DS₂-VASc 0 for men, 1 for women): Cardioversion can be performed without prior anticoagulation. Start anticoagulation after cardioversion based on CHA₂DS₂-VASc.
  • AF onset < 48 hours & higher stroke risk: Offer anticoagulation at presentation. Cardioversion can proceed. Continue anticoagulation for at least 4 weeks post-cardioversion, then reassess.
  • AF onset > 48 hours or uncertain: Anticoagulate for at least 3 weeks before cardioversion OR perform TOE to exclude left atrial appendage thrombus. Continue anticoagulation for at least 4 weeks post-cardioversion, then reassess.

Methods:

Electrical Cardioversion (DCCV)

Preferred if patient is haemodynamically unstable or if pharmacological cardioversion is contraindicated/failed. Usually requires sedation.

Pharmacological Cardioversion

No structural heart disease: Flecainide (IV or oral). Caution if suspected atrial flutter.

Structural heart disease (e.g., IHD, valvular disease, LV impairment): Amiodarone (IV). Slower onset.

Beta-blocker co-prescription may be needed with flecainide if risk of 1:1 flutter conduction.

Rate Control Strategy

  • Initial Target Heart Rate: <110 bpm at rest. If symptoms persist or heart failure, aim for <80 bpm.
  • First-Line Agents (monotherapy):
    • Beta-blocker (e.g., bisoprolol, metoprolol). Avoid in severe asthma or decompensated heart failure.
    • Rate-limiting Calcium Channel Blocker (e.g., diltiazem, verapamil). Avoid in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF).
  • Second-Line / Combination: If monotherapy fails, consider adding Digoxin. Specialist advice may be needed for combining beta-blocker and CCB.
  • Heart Failure (HFrEF): Beta-blocker (once stable) or Digoxin. Avoid CCBs. Amiodarone can be used for rate control under specialist advice if other agents unsuitable/ineffective.

Anticoagulation for Stroke Prevention

  • Assess Stroke Risk: Use CHA₂DS₂-VASc score.
    • Score 0 (men) or 1 (women): Anticoagulation generally not indicated unless AF due to reversible cause and cardioversion planned.
    • Score ≥1 (men) or ≥2 (women): Offer anticoagulation. (Note: NICE threshold is CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥2 for offering, but consider for men with 1). For practical ED purposes, if CHA₂DS₂-VASc indicates need, start or ensure plan.

  • Assess Bleeding Risk: Use ORBIT score.

    Older Age (≥75 years)1 point
    Reduced Hb/Hct/Anaemia history2 points
    Bleeding History (major)2 points
    Insufficient Renal Function (eGFR <60)1 point
    Treatment with Antiplatelet1 point
    Score 0-2: Low risk
    Score 3: Medium risk
    Score 4-7: High risk

    A high ORBIT score prompts caution, review of modifiable bleeding risk factors, and discussion. It is not an absolute contraindication to anticoagulation for most patients.

  • Choice of Anticoagulant:
    • DOACs (apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban, rivaroxaban) are generally preferred over warfarin. Consider patient factors, renal function, and interactions.
    • Warfarin if DOACs contraindicated or not suitable.
  • When to Start: Initiate as soon as possible once decision to anticoagulate is made, including in the ED if appropriate and safe, or ensure clear plan for initiation.

Admission & Referral

Consider ADMISSION if:

  • Haemodynamically unstable.
  • Serious underlying cause requiring admission (e.g., sepsis, PE, ACS).
  • AF with features of Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome (seek urgent cardiology advice).
  • Uncontrolled symptoms despite initial ED management.
  • High risk of stroke and anticoagulation needs urgent inpatient initiation/bridging.
  • Significant comorbidities complicating management.
  • Uncertainty about diagnosis or management plan.

Consider DISCHARGE with Follow-Up if:

  • Haemodynamically stable.
  • Symptoms controlled (rate or rhythm).
  • Underlying cause addressed or not acute.
  • Appropriate anticoagulation plan in place (initiated or clear follow-up for initiation).
  • Clear follow-up arranged (e.g., GP, anticoagulation clinic, cardiology outpatient within appropriate timeframe).
  • Patient understands plan and red flags.
